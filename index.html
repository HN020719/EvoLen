EvoLen: Evolution-Guided Tokenization for DNA Language Model

DNA has no natural word boundaries. EvoLen uses evolutionary constraint to discover sequence units that better preserve biological function.

01 / THE METHOD

Tokenization is a
biological inductive bias.

Instead of relying only on sequence frequency, EvoLen incorporates evolutionary information into vocabulary construction and length-aware decoding.

01

Stratify

Use phyloP to separate conserved, neutral, and accelerated genomic regions.

02

Build & merge

Learn category-specific BPE vocabularies, then prioritize conserved sequence patterns.

03

Decode

Score tokens by length squared. Dynamic programming finds the highest-scoring segmentation.

02 / BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE

What should a genomic
token preserve?

Four properties connect tokenizer design to biological structure. EvoLen is evaluated against standard BPE.

P1 Functional integrity

Keep motifs intact.

+27.4%

Higher motif perfect match rate at vocabulary size 5,120.

P2 Regulatory specificity

Distinguish genomic contexts.

+41–53%

Greater separation between region-specific token-length distributions.

P3 Evolutionary consistency

Align with constraint.

Up to +14.4%

Higher mean token phyloP. This tests alignment with a signal also used in construction.

P4 Pattern recurrence

Capture context-specific
functional patterns.

EvoLen shows stronger context-specific enrichment relative to a neutral intronic background. Conserved–accelerated separation increases for enhancers, exons, and introns; promoter separation remains comparable.

03 / DOWNSTREAM PERFORMANCE

Scope & limitations

Gains are less consistent for precise boundary recognition, such as splice sites, and for species outside the mammalian evolutionary signal used in construction. Robustness is assessed across tasks rather than multiple pretraining seeds. The aggregate 56-task statistics above refer to the 100K comparison.
